# Simple Machines
Tools that make work easier by changing forces.

## What is a machine?
A **machine** is any tool that helps us do work. In science, "work" means using a force to move something. **Simple machines** use just one or two parts to make work easier — either by changing the direction of a force, or by making a small force do the job of a big one.

## The six simple machines

### 1. Lever
A **lever** is a bar that pivots on a fixed point called a **fulcrum**.

Examples: seesaw, crowbar, scissors, bottle opener

If you put the fulcrum near the object you want to lift, a small push on the other end lifts a heavy load. That's the magic of a lever.

### 2. Inclined Plane
An **inclined plane** is a flat surface set at an angle — a **ramp**.

Instead of lifting something straight up, you push it up the ramp. It takes less force but the distance is longer. That's a fair trade.

Examples: wheelchair ramps, roads up a hill, playground slides

### 3. Wedge
A **wedge** is two inclined planes joined together. It pushes things apart.

Examples: axe, knife, chisel, nail, teeth

A wedge works by concentrating force on a small point. That's why a sharp knife cuts more easily than a dull one.

### 4. Pulley
A **pulley** is a wheel with a rope or cable running over it. It changes the direction of a force, and with more pulleys, it can reduce the force needed to lift heavy things.

Examples: flagpole, crane, blinds, well bucket

### 5. Wheel and Axle
A **wheel and axle** is a wheel attached to a rod. Turning the wheel turns the axle, or vice versa.

Examples: doorknob, screwdriver, bicycle wheel, steering wheel

### 6. Screw
A **screw** is an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder. It converts turning force into forward force.

Examples: bolts, jar lids, water bottle caps, spiral staircase

## Simple vs compound machines
A **compound machine** is made of two or more simple machines working together. A bicycle, for example, uses:
- Wheels and axles
- Levers (brakes and pedals)
- Pulleys (chain and gears)

A wheelbarrow combines a lever and a wheel and axle.

## Why machines matter
Simple machines have been used for thousands of years — long before electricity or engines. Pyramids were built with inclined planes and levers. Ships were loaded with pulleys. Fields were plowed with wedges. Even today, every machine — from a bicycle to a spacecraft — is built on these six basic ideas.
