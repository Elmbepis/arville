# The Respiratory System
How your body brings in oxygen and removes carbon dioxide.

## Why we breathe
Every cell in your body needs **oxygen** to make energy from food. Breathing brings oxygen in and carries **carbon dioxide** — a waste gas — out.

You breathe about **20,000 times a day** without thinking about it.

## The journey of air

### 1. Nose and mouth
Air enters through the **nose** or the **mouth**. The nose is better because it:
- **Filters** dust and germs with tiny hairs
- **Warms** the air to body temperature
- **Moistens** the air so it doesn't dry your lungs

### 2. Pharynx and larynx
Air passes through the **pharynx** (throat) and **larynx** (voice box). The larynx holds your **vocal cords** — the muscles that vibrate to make sound.

### 3. Trachea (windpipe)
Air travels down the **trachea**, a tube about 10–12 cm long. Rings of cartilage keep it open so it doesn't collapse.

A small flap called the **epiglottis** closes when you swallow, so food goes down the esophagus instead of into your lungs.

### 4. Bronchi
The trachea splits into two tubes called **bronchi** — one going to each lung.

### 5. Lungs and bronchioles
Inside the lungs, the bronchi branch into smaller and smaller tubes called **bronchioles** — like an upside-down tree.

### 6. Alveoli
At the end of the bronchioles are tiny balloon-like sacs called **alveoli**. There are about **300 million** of them in each lung. Here's where the real magic happens:
- **Oxygen** passes from the air into your blood
- **Carbon dioxide** passes from your blood into the air to be breathed out

## The diaphragm
Below your lungs is a dome-shaped muscle called the **diaphragm**.
- When it contracts, it moves down and pulls air **in**
- When it relaxes, it moves up and pushes air **out**

You don't have to think about it — your brainstem does the job automatically.

## What happens when you exercise
When you run or play:
- Your muscles need more oxygen
- Your breathing gets faster and deeper
- Your heart pumps faster to move oxygen around
- You might feel out of breath for a moment afterward

That's your body's way of catching up.

## Common breathing problems
- **Asthma** — airways tighten and swell, making it hard to breathe
- **Colds and flu** — mucus fills the airways
- **Smoking** — damages alveoli and can cause lung cancer

## Keeping your lungs healthy
- **Don't smoke** — and stay away from secondhand smoke
- **Exercise** — strengthens breathing muscles
- **Breathe clean air** — avoid dust and pollution
- **Cover your mouth** when you cough or sneeze

## Try this at home
Put your hand on your chest. Breathe in slowly through your nose for 4 counts. Feel your chest rise. Now breathe out slowly through your mouth for 6 counts. Do this 5 times. That's your diaphragm at work.
