# The Endocrine System
How hormones control your body from the inside.

## What the endocrine system is
The **endocrine system** is a network of **glands** that make and release **hormones**. Hormones are chemical messengers. They travel through your blood to tell different parts of your body what to do.

If the nervous system is like a text message (fast, direct), the endocrine system is like a letter — slower, but reaching far and lasting longer.

## What hormones do
Hormones control almost everything that happens slowly in your body:
- How fast you grow
- How much energy you have
- When you feel hungry or full
- How your body responds to stress
- When you go through puberty
- How your body uses sugar

## The main glands

### Pituitary gland
The **pituitary** is a pea-sized gland at the base of your brain. It's often called the **"master gland"** because it tells other glands what to do.
- Controls growth
- Tells the thyroid, adrenal glands, and reproductive organs when to work

### Thyroid gland
The **thyroid** is a butterfly-shaped gland in your neck.
- Controls how fast your body uses energy (metabolism)
- Helps control body temperature and weight

### Pancreas
The **pancreas** is behind your stomach. It has two jobs:
- Makes **insulin** — a hormone that controls blood sugar
- Makes digestive enzymes (that part belongs to the digestive system)

If the pancreas doesn't make enough insulin, sugar builds up in the blood. This is called **diabetes**.

### Adrenal glands
The **adrenal glands** sit on top of your kidneys.
- Make **adrenaline** — the "fight or flight" hormone
- When you're scared or excited, adrenaline makes your heart pound, breathing quicken, and muscles ready to act

### Reproductive glands
- **Ovaries** in females make estrogen
- **Testes** in males make testosterone
- These hormones control growth during puberty and the ability to have children

## How hormones work
1. A gland makes a hormone
2. The hormone enters the blood
3. Blood carries it to a target organ
4. The target organ responds

Hormones are incredibly powerful. A tiny amount — millionths of a gram — can change how your whole body behaves.

## When hormones change
Sometimes hormones change suddenly, and you can feel it:
- **Puberty** — hormones cause your body to grow and change
- **Stress** — adrenaline makes your heart race
- **Hunger** — ghrelin (a hormone) tells you it's time to eat
- **Fullness** — leptin (another hormone) tells you to stop
- **Sleep** — melatonin helps you feel sleepy at night

## Keeping your endocrine system healthy
- **Sleep well** — hormones balance during sleep
- **Eat well** — too much sugar stresses the pancreas
- **Exercise** — helps hormones work properly
- **Manage stress** — too much stress tires the adrenal glands
- **Avoid harmful chemicals** — some can disrupt hormones

## Try this at home
Think about a time you were suddenly scared or excited — maybe a surprise, a loud noise, or a fast ride. Remember how your heart pounded and you felt alert? That was your adrenal glands releasing adrenaline, getting your body ready to react.
