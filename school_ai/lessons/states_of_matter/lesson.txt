# States of Matter
Solids, liquids, gases — and how they change.

## Three states
Everything around you is made of **matter**. Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space. Matter comes in three main states: **solid**, **liquid**, and **gas**.

## Solids
A solid has a **definite shape** and a **definite volume**. If you put a rock in a box, it stays a rock. If you move the box, the rock doesn't change shape. Books, chairs, ice cubes, and pencils are all solids.

## Liquids
A liquid has **no definite shape**, but it has a **definite volume**. Pour water into a glass — it takes the shape of the glass. Pour the same water into a bottle — it takes the shape of the bottle. But the amount of water doesn't change. Milk, juice, and oil are liquids.

## Gases
A gas has **no definite shape** and **no definite volume**. Gas spreads out to fill whatever space it's in. Air is a mixture of gases. If you open a bottle of perfume, the smell spreads through the room — that's gas spreading out.

## Changing state
Matter can change from one state to another when heat is added or removed:

- **Melting** — solid to liquid (ice melts into water)
- **Freezing** — liquid to solid (water freezes into ice)
- **Evaporation** — liquid to gas (water dries up in the sun)
- **Condensation** — gas to liquid (water droplets form on a cold glass)

Heat speeds up the tiny particles inside matter. When particles move faster, matter can change state.

## The water cycle
These changes happen all around us in the **water cycle**. The Sun heats water in oceans and lakes, causing **evaporation**. The water vapor rises and cools, forming clouds through **condensation**. When the clouds get heavy, water falls as rain. This rain flows back into the oceans and lakes, and the cycle starts again.

## Measuring volume
Scientists measure the volume of liquids in **milliliters (mL)** and **liters (L)**. A small bottle of water might be 500 mL. A big container of juice might be 2 L.
