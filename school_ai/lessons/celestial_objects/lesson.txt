# Objects in the Sky
The Sun, Moon, planets, and stars.

## Looking up
When you look at the sky, you can see many natural objects. Some are visible during the day, some at night, and some both. These are called **celestial objects** — natural things that exist in space.

## The Sun
The **Sun** is a huge ball of hot, glowing gas. It is about 100 times wider than the Earth. It looks small only because it is very far away — about 150 million kilometers from us.

The Sun is our main source of light and heat. Without it, Earth would be dark, frozen, and lifeless.

**Fun fact:** The Sun is a star — the closest star to Earth. It just looks bigger and brighter than other stars because it is closer.

## The Moon
The **Moon** is Earth's only natural satellite. It orbits the Earth once every 29 days.

The Moon does not make its own light. It **reflects** light from the Sun. That is why it looks bright in the night sky.

The Moon has **phases** — different shapes we see at different times:
- **New Moon** — the Moon is between the Earth and the Sun, so we see its dark side. It looks invisible.
- **Crescent** — a thin sliver is lit
- **Quarter** — half of the Moon's face is lit
- **Gibbous** — more than half is lit
- **Full Moon** — the whole face is lit

The Moon's gravity causes **tides** in the ocean. It also causes **solar and lunar eclipses**.

## The Planets
**Planets** are large round objects that orbit a star. Our Sun has eight planets. Here are all of them, in order from the Sun:

### Mercury
Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun and the smallest planet in our solar system. It has almost no atmosphere, so its days are blazing hot (up to 430°C) and its nights are freezing cold (down to −180°C). Mercury has no moons and completes one trip around the Sun in just 88 Earth days.

### Venus
Venus is the second planet from the Sun and the hottest planet of all. It is covered in thick clouds of gas that trap heat, making its surface about 465°C — hot enough to melt lead. Venus is about the same size as Earth and is sometimes called "Earth's twin," but its thick atmosphere makes it a very different and dangerous place.

### Earth
Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only planet known to have life. It has liquid water, an atmosphere we can breathe, and a temperature range that supports plants, animals, and people. Earth has one moon, which we simply call the Moon, and takes 365 days to orbit the Sun.

### Mars
Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and is called the "Red Planet" because of the iron-rich dust that covers its surface. It has the tallest volcano in the solar system (Olympus Mons) and the largest canyon (Valles Marineris). Scientists have sent many robots to explore Mars, looking for signs that water — and possibly life — once existed there.

### Jupiter
Jupiter is the fifth planet and the largest in our solar system — more than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it. It is a giant ball of gas with a famous swirling storm called the Great Red Spot, which has been raging for hundreds of years. Jupiter has at least 95 moons, including Ganymede, which is bigger than the planet Mercury.

### Saturn
Saturn is the sixth planet and the second largest in our solar system. It is famous for its beautiful rings, which are made of billions of pieces of ice and rock. Saturn has more than 140 known moons, including Titan, which has lakes and rivers — of liquid methane, not water.

### Uranus
Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun and is a cold, blue-green gas giant. It spins on its side, likely knocked over by an ancient collision long ago, so its seasons are extreme — each pole faces the Sun for 42 Earth years at a time. Uranus has faint rings and at least 27 moons.

### Neptune
Neptune is the eighth and farthest planet from the Sun. It is a deep blue gas giant with the strongest winds in the solar system — over 2,000 kilometers per hour. Neptune was the first planet discovered by mathematical prediction rather than by telescope observation, when astronomers noticed Uranus was being pulled by an unseen world.

**Order to remember:** My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Noodles — Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.

## Stars
**Stars** are huge balls of burning gas, like the Sun but much farther away. They make their own light.

When you look at the night sky, you see thousands of stars. Our galaxy, the **Milky Way**, contains billions of stars.

Stars have different brightness and color. Blue stars are hottest. Red stars are coolest.

## How objects move across the sky
Everything in the sky seems to rise in the **east** and set in the **west**. But it is not the sky moving — it is the Earth **rotating**. As the Earth spins, different parts of the sky come into view.

The Moon moves a bit differently — it rises about 50 minutes later each night.

## Sun safety
The Sun is powerful. Too much sun can hurt your skin and eyes.
- Wear a hat and sunglasses
- Use sunscreen
- Stay in the shade during the hottest part of the day
- **Never look directly at the Sun** — it can damage your eyes permanently

## Try this at home
Look at the Moon for several nights in a row. Draw its shape each night. After a week, you will see it change from a thin crescent to a fuller shape (or the reverse). That is the Moon's phases.
