# Metals
The shiny materials that build our tools, wires, and buildings.

## What is a metal?
A metal is a material found in the ground. Metals are usually **shiny**, **hard**, and can be shaped without breaking. Look around your home or classroom and you'll find metals everywhere: the frame of your chair, the coins in your pocket, the wiring in the walls, even inside your phone.

## Common metals and where they come from
- **Iron** — used to make steel, which builds bridges, cars, and buildings
- **Copper** — a reddish-brown metal, used in electrical wires because electricity flows through it easily
- **Aluminum** — light and silver-colored, used for soda cans, foil, and airplane parts
- **Gold** — shiny yellow metal, used for jewelry and in electronics
- **Silver** — the best conductor of electricity, used in coins, mirrors, and electronics

## How we get metals
Metals are found inside **ores** — rocks that contain metal mixed with other materials. People dig these rocks out of the ground in mines. Then they heat the ore in a very hot furnace to separate the metal from the rock. This is called **smelting**.

## Properties of metals
- **Shiny** — most metals reflect light
- **Good conductors** — heat and electricity travel through them easily
- **Malleable** — they can be hammered flat without shattering
- **Ductile** — they can be pulled into thin wires
- **Solid at room temperature** — except mercury, which is a liquid metal

## Metals in your community
Look around your neighborhood. You'll see metals in tricycle parts, jeepney bodies, sari-sari store shelves, gates, fences, cooking pots, and in the wires bringing electricity to your home. In the Philippines, some communities also mine copper, gold, and nickel.

## Why metals matter
Metals help us build, travel, cook, communicate, and light our homes. Without metals, we wouldn't have electricity, cars, buildings with strong frames, or most of the tools we use every day. But mining and using metals can also hurt the environment if done carelessly, so we need to recycle them and use them wisely.
