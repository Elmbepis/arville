# Invertebrates
Animals without backbones — the largest group on Earth.

## What is an invertebrate?
An **invertebrate** is an animal that does **not** have a backbone. No spine, no skeleton of bones inside its body. Instead, many invertebrates have a hard outer covering called an **exoskeleton**, or they have soft bodies.

About 96 out of every 100 animals on Earth are invertebrates. That means when you see an animal, it's far more likely to be an invertebrate than a vertebrate.

## The main groups of invertebrates

### Insects
Insects have six legs and three body parts: head, thorax, and abdomen. Most have wings. Examples: ants, bees, butterflies, beetles, crickets.

### Arachnids
Arachnids have eight legs and two body parts. They do not have wings. Examples: spiders, scorpions, ticks, mites.

### Mollusks
Mollusks have soft bodies, and many have a hard shell. Examples: snails, clams, oysters, octopuses, squids.

### Crustaceans
Crustaceans have hard shells and many legs. Most live in water. Examples: crabs, shrimp, lobsters, crayfish.

### Worms
Worms have long, soft bodies with no legs. Examples: earthworms, leeches, tapeworms.

## Where invertebrates live
Invertebrates live almost everywhere — in soil, water, air, on plants, on animals, and inside other animals. In one square meter of garden soil, there can be thousands of them.

## Why invertebrates matter
Even though they are small, invertebrates do huge jobs:
- **Bees and butterflies** pollinate flowers so fruits and seeds can grow
- **Earthworms** loosen the soil so plant roots can grow
- **Spiders** eat insects that would otherwise damage crops
- **Crabs and shrimp** are food for fish, birds, and people
- **Snails and slugs** break down dead leaves

Without invertebrates, food chains would collapse. Plants would not get pollinated. Soil would not be healthy. Life as we know it would stop.

## Invertebrates vs vertebrates
| Feature | Vertebrates | Invertebrates |
|---|---|---|
| Backbone | Yes | No |
| Skeleton | Inside the body | Outside (exoskeleton) or none |
| Body size | Usually larger | Usually smaller |
| Number of species | About 4% of animals | About 96% of animals |
| Examples | Dogs, birds, fish, frogs | Insects, spiders, worms, snails |

## Try this at home
Go outside and gently turn over a rock or a fallen leaf. Count the invertebrates you see — ants, worms, beetles, spiders. How many different types can you find? Put the rock back carefully so their home stays intact.
