# Vertebrates
Animals with backbones — and the five main groups.

## What is a vertebrate?
A **vertebrate** is an animal that has a **backbone**. The backbone is a column of small bones that runs down the middle of the animal's back. It supports the body and protects the spinal cord, which carries messages between the brain and the rest of the body.

Animals without a backbone are called **invertebrates**. Insects, worms, snails, and spiders are all invertebrates. About 96% of all animal species are invertebrates!

## The five groups of vertebrates

### Mammals
- Have **fur or hair**
- Feed their babies **milk** from their mother's body
- Are **warm-blooded** (their body stays the same temperature)
- Breathe with **lungs**
- Examples: humans, dogs, cats, whales, bats

### Birds
- Have **feathers**
- Lay **eggs**
- Have **wings** and most can fly
- Are **warm-blooded**
- Breathe with **lungs**
- Examples: eagles, chickens, parrots, penguins

### Fish
- Live in **water**
- Breathe with **gills**
- Have **scales** and **fins**
- Are **cold-blooded** (their body temperature changes with the water)
- Examples: tilapia, tuna, sharks, goldfish

### Reptiles
- Have **dry, scaly skin**
- Lay **eggs** on land
- Are **cold-blooded**
- Breathe with **lungs**
- Examples: snakes, lizards, turtles, crocodiles

### Amphibians
- Spend part of their life in **water** and part on **land**
- Have **moist skin**
- Lay eggs in water
- Are **cold-blooded**
- Start life with gills, then develop lungs
- Examples: frogs, toads, salamanders

## How to tell them apart
If you see a new animal, ask yourself:
1. Does it have feathers? → **Bird**
2. Does it have fur, and does it feed its young milk? → **Mammal**
3. Does it live in water and have gills? → **Fish**
4. Does it have dry scales and lay eggs on land? → **Reptile**
5. Does it live in water as a baby and on land as an adult? → **Amphibian**

## Try this at home
Look at pictures of five animals. For each one, guess which vertebrate group it belongs to. Then check: Does it have feathers, fur, scales, or moist skin? That's your best clue.
