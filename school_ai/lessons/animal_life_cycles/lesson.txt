# Animal Life Cycles
How animals grow and change from birth to adulthood.

## What is a life cycle?
A **life cycle** is the series of stages an animal goes through from the moment it's born (or hatched) until it becomes an adult that can have its own young. Many animals look completely different at different stages.

## Metamorphosis
Some animals go through dramatic changes as they grow. This is called **metamorphosis**. There are two types.

## Complete metamorphosis: the butterfly
Butterflies go through four stages:

1. **Egg** — laid on a leaf
2. **Larva** (caterpillar) — hatches and eats leaves, growing fast
3. **Pupa** (chrysalis) — the caterpillar wraps itself up and changes completely
4. **Adult butterfly** — emerges with wings, ready to lay eggs

The young look nothing like the adult. This is **complete metamorphosis**.

## Incomplete metamorphosis: the frog
Frogs also change a lot, but differently:

1. **Egg** — laid in water in a jelly-like cluster
2. **Tadpole** — hatches with a tail and gills, swims like a fish
3. **Froglet** — grows legs, loses gills, starts to breathe air
4. **Adult frog** — lives on land and in water, can lay eggs

## Life cycles without metamorphosis
Many animals don't change shape as they grow. They just get bigger. Examples:

### Chickens
- **Egg** → **Chick** → **Adult chicken**
- The chick looks like a smaller version of the adult, covered in soft down.

### Humans
- **Baby** → **Child** → **Teenager** → **Adult**
- We grow and change, but we always look human.

### Mammals
- **Baby** → **Young** → **Adult**
- Mammals feed their babies milk until they can eat solid food.

## Comparing the stages
| Animal | Stages | Looks like adult? |
|---|---|---|
| Butterfly | 4 | No — completely different |
| Frog | 4 | No — tadpole looks like a fish |
| Chicken | 3 | Yes — just smaller |
| Human | 4 | Yes — just smaller |
| Dog | 3 | Yes — just smaller |

## Why life cycles matter
Understanding life cycles helps us:
- Protect young animals during their fragile stages
- Know when animals breed and when to avoid disturbing them
- Understand why some species are more vulnerable than others

For example, frogs need water to lay eggs. If their pond dries up, no tadpoles will hatch, and no new frogs will grow.

## Try this at home
Look for a caterpillar on a leafy plant. Watch it for a few days. Does it move? Does it eat? If you find a chrysalis, leave it alone — a butterfly is forming inside.
