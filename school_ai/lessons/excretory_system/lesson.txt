# The Excretory System
How your body removes waste from the inside.

## What the excretory system does
Your body makes waste all day long. Cells produce leftover materials that would poison you if they stayed inside. The **excretory system** collects these wastes and removes them from the body.

The main wastes are:
- **Carbon dioxide** — removed by the lungs
- **Urea and extra salts** — removed by the urinary system
- **Extra water and salt** — removed in sweat
- **Undigested food** — removed by the digestive system

## The main organs

### Kidneys
Two bean-shaped organs that filter your blood. They remove waste, extra water, and extra salts as **urine**. Each kidney contains about a million tiny filters called **nephrons**.

[[urinary_system|Learn more about the urinary system →]]

### Lungs
The lungs remove **carbon dioxide** — a waste gas made when cells use oxygen to make energy.

### Skin
The **skin** removes small amounts of water, salt, and urea when you sweat. It also helps cool your body.

### Liver
The **liver** breaks down harmful substances (like old blood cells) and turns them into urea. The kidneys then filter that urea out.

## Why water matters
Your kidneys need water to work. If you don't drink enough:
- Urine becomes dark and strong-smelling
- Waste builds up in your blood
- You can get kidney stones

Drinking 6–8 glasses of water a day keeps your kidneys happy.

## Keeping your excretory system healthy
- **Drink water** — helps flush out waste
- **Eat less salt** — too much salt makes the kidneys work harder
- **Don't hold urine too long** — can lead to infections
- **Avoid too much sugar and junk food** — can damage kidneys over time
