# The Urinary System
How your body filters blood and makes urine.

## What the urinary system does
The **urinary system** cleans your blood. It removes waste, extra water, and extra salts. What's left over is **urine**, which leaves the body.

The urinary system is the main part of the larger excretory system — the body's entire waste-removal team.

## The four parts

### 1. Kidneys
You have two **kidneys**, shaped like beans, one on each side of your lower back. They are the body's filters.

- Each kidney contains about a **million tiny filters** called **nephrons**
- Together, your kidneys clean about **180 liters of blood every day** — enough to fill a small bathtub
- They remove waste, extra water, and extra salts as **urine**
- They also balance salt, water, and acid in your blood

Even **one healthy kidney** can do the job of two. That's why some people can donate a kidney and still live a normal life.

### 2. Ureters
Two thin tubes called **ureters** carry urine from each kidney down to the bladder. Each ureter is about 25–30 cm long.

### 3. Bladder
The **bladder** is a stretchy, muscular bag that stores urine.
- When empty, it's about the size of a fist
- When full, it can hold about 400–600 mL (roughly 2 cups)
- When it fills up, nerves tell your brain it's time to go
- Muscles in the bladder wall squeeze to push urine out

### 4. Urethra
The **urethra** is the tube that carries urine out of the body. Muscles called **sphincters** control when urine leaves. In females the urethra is short (about 4 cm); in males it is longer (about 20 cm).

## What urine is made of
Urine is mostly water — about 95%. The rest is:
- **Urea** — a waste from broken-down proteins
- **Extra salts** — sodium, potassium, chloride
- **Extra water** — whatever the body doesn't need
- **Small amounts of other waste**

Urine is usually pale yellow. Darker urine means you need to drink more water.

## How the kidneys filter blood
1. Blood flows into the kidney through the **renal artery**
2. Tiny filters (nephrons) separate waste from useful materials
3. Useful materials (water, glucose, salts) return to the blood
4. Waste and extra water become urine
5. Clean blood leaves through the **renal vein**
6. Urine flows down the ureter to the bladder

## Common urinary system problems
- **Urinary tract infection (UTI)** — germs in the bladder or urethra cause burning and frequent urination
- **Kidney stones** — hard crystals of minerals that hurt when they pass
- **Kidney disease** — kidneys slowly lose their ability to filter

## Keeping your urinary system healthy
- **Drink plenty of water** — 6–8 glasses a day
- **Don't hold urine too long** — can lead to infections
- **Eat less salt** — too much salt makes the kidneys work harder
- **Avoid too much sugar and junk food**
- **Wash regularly** — keeps germs away from the urethra

## Try this at home
Notice the color of your urine through the day:
- **Pale yellow** — well hydrated ✅
- **Dark yellow** — drink more water
- **Very dark or red** — tell an adult
