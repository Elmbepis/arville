# Animal Adaptations
How animals change to survive in their environments.

## What is an adaptation?
An **adaptation** is a body part or behavior that helps an animal survive. Adaptations develop slowly over many generations. Animals that have helpful adaptations live longer and have more young. Animals without them do not survive as well.

There are two main types of adaptations: **body adaptations** and **behavior adaptations**.

## Body adaptations

### Camouflage
Camouflage means blending in with the surroundings. A green praying mantis on a leaf, a brown lizard on a rock, a white polar bear on snow — all are hard to see, which helps them hide from predators or sneak up on prey.

Examples:
- **Stick insects** look like twigs
- **Leaf insects** look like leaves
- **Arctic foxes** turn white in winter, brown in summer
- **Chameleons** change color to match their surroundings

### Mimicry
Mimicry means looking or sounding like something else — usually something dangerous. A harmless hoverfly has yellow and black stripes, just like a stinging wasp. Predators avoid it because they think it stings.

Examples:
- **Viceroy butterflies** look like poisonous monarch butterflies
- **Some frogs** imitate the call of dangerous species
- **Octopuses** can change shape, color, and texture to look like rocks or coral

### Special body parts
- **Beaks** — eagles have sharp hooked beaks for tearing meat; hummingbirds have long thin beaks for sipping nectar
- **Claws** — cats have sharp retractable claws for catching prey
- **Fins** — fish have fins for swimming
- **Gills** — fish and tadpoles breathe underwater with gills
- **Humps** — camels store fat in their humps to survive long desert trips
- **Thick fur** — animals in cold places have thick fur to stay warm

## Behavior adaptations

### Migration
Some animals travel long distances to find food or warmer weather. Birds like the Arctic tern fly from pole to pole every year. Whales swim thousands of kilometers to feeding grounds.

### Hibernation
Some animals sleep through the cold months when food is scarce. Bears, bats, and some frogs hibernate. Their bodies slow down — heart rate drops, breathing slows, body temperature falls — so they need less energy.

### Living in groups
Many animals live in groups for safety. Fish swim in schools. Birds flock together. Buffaloes travel in herds. Being in a group means more eyes watching for danger.

### Building homes
Beavers build dams. Birds build nests. Ants build underground tunnels. Spiders spin webs. These homes protect them from weather and predators.

## How adaptations develop
Adaptations are not planned. They happen through **natural selection**:
1. Animals in a species are slightly different from each other
2. Some differences help certain animals survive better
3. Those animals live longer and have more young
4. Over many generations, the helpful traits spread

For example, long-beaked hummingbirds could reach more nectar than short-beaked ones — so long-beaked birds survived better, and the species gradually became long-beaked.
