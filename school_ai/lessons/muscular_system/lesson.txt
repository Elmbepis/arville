# The Muscular System
The muscles that move your bones and keep your body working.

## What muscles do
**Muscles** are tissues that can tighten (contract) and relax. When they contract, they pull on bones and create movement. Your body has more than **600 muscles**.

Muscles do more than just move you — they:
- Help you stand, sit, walk, and run
- Pump blood (the heart is a muscle)
- Move food through your digestive system
- Help you breathe
- Keep your body warm by shivering

## Two types of muscles

### Voluntary muscles
**Voluntary** muscles are the ones you control on purpose.
- Lifting your arm
- Smiling
- Walking
- Writing

These are also called **skeletal muscles** because they attach to bones.

### Involuntary muscles
**Involuntary** muscles work without you thinking about them.
- Your heart beating
- Your stomach digesting food
- Your intestines moving food through
- Your pupils widening in the dark

You can't control these even if you try.

## How muscles move bones
Muscles work in **pairs**. When one muscle contracts (shortens), the other relaxes (lengthens). Then they switch.

Example: your upper arm has two main muscles:
- **Biceps** — on the front; contracts to bend your arm
- **Triceps** — on the back; contracts to straighten your arm

When you lift a book, your biceps contract and your triceps relax. When you put the book down, they switch.

## Where muscles attach
Muscles attach to bones with strong cords called **tendons**.
- **Achilles tendon** — connects your calf muscle to your heel
- **Biceps tendon** — connects your biceps to your shoulder

When you pull a muscle, it's often a tendon or muscle fiber that's torn.

## Big muscles and small muscles
- **Gluteus maximus** — your biggest muscle, in your buttocks; helps you stand and run
- **Stapedius** — your smallest muscle, in your ear; less than 2 mm long

## Keeping muscles healthy
- **Exercise** — running, swimming, and sports build strength
- **Eat protein** — from eggs, meat, fish, beans, and nuts
- **Stretch** — keeps muscles flexible
- **Drink water** — muscles need water to work well
- **Rest** — muscles repair themselves when you sleep
- **Warm up** — before sports, to prevent injuries

## Muscle cramps
A **cramp** is a sudden, painful tightening of a muscle. It often happens when:
- You exercise hard without warming up
- You're low on water or salt
- You hold a position too long

Stretching and drinking water usually helps.

## Try this at home
Place your hand on your upper arm and bend your elbow. Feel the muscle bulge — that's your biceps contracting. Now straighten your arm. The bulge moves to the back — that's your triceps working.
