# Measurement
How we measure length, mass, volume, and temperature.

## Why we measure
**Measurement** tells us "how much" — how long, how heavy, how hot, how full. Scientists measure things to compare them, to record results, and to repeat experiments accurately. Without measurement, science is just guessing.

## Length and distance
We measure length using a **ruler**, **meterstick**, or **tape measure**.

Units of length, from smallest to largest:
- **Millimeter (mm)** — about the thickness of a coin
- **Centimeter (cm)** — about the width of your finger (10 mm = 1 cm)
- **Meter (m)** — about the height of a door (100 cm = 1 m)
- **Kilometer (km)** — a walk of about 10 minutes (1,000 m = 1 km)

## Mass (weight)
We measure mass using a **balance** or **weighing scale**.

Units of mass:
- **Milligram (mg)** — very tiny, like a grain of salt
- **Gram (g)** — about a paperclip (1,000 mg = 1 g)
- **Kilogram (kg)** — about a bag of rice (1,000 g = 1 kg)

## Volume (how much space)
We measure the volume of liquids using a **measuring cylinder** or **beaker**.

Units of volume:
- **Milliliter (mL)** — about a teaspoon (used for small amounts)
- **Liter (L)** — about a big bottle of water (1,000 mL = 1 L)

For solids, we measure volume in **cubic centimeters (cm³)**. A cube 1 cm on every side has a volume of 1 cm³ — and holds exactly 1 mL of water.

## Temperature
We measure temperature using a **thermometer**.

The unit is **degrees Celsius (°C)**:
- **0°C** — water freezes
- **25°C** — a warm room
- **37°C** — normal body temperature
- **100°C** — water boils

In science, we always say the unit. "It is 20" means nothing — "it is 20 °C" or "20 cm" or "20 g" tells us what we measured.

## Time
We measure time using a **clock**, **stopwatch**, or **timer**.

Units of time:
- **Second (s)** — a quick blink
- **Minute (min)** — 60 seconds
- **Hour (h)** — 60 minutes
- **Day** — 24 hours
- **Year** — about 365 days

## Estimating vs measuring
**Estimating** is guessing a measurement without a tool — "the table looks about 1 meter wide." **Measuring** is checking with a tool — "the table is 97 cm wide." Estimation is useful when you don't have a tool, but for science, always measure.

## Accuracy tips
- Start measuring from the **0 mark**, not the edge of the ruler
- Keep the ruler **flat** against what you're measuring
- Read at **eye level** to avoid errors
- For liquids, read the bottom of the **meniscus** (the curve of the liquid)
- Record the number and the **unit**

## Try this at home
Use a ruler to measure five things in your home: a pencil, a book, a spoon, a shoe, and the width of your thumb. Write down the measurements in centimeters. Which is longest? Which is shortest?
