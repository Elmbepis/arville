# Photosynthesis
How plants make their own food using sunlight.

## The big idea
Plants don't eat food the way animals do. Instead, they make their own food inside their leaves, using energy from the Sun. This process is called **photosynthesis**. The word means "putting together with light."

## What plants need
Three things go into a leaf to make food:
- **Sunlight** — captured by the leaf
- **Water** — pulled up from the soil through the roots and stem
- **Carbon dioxide** — a gas from the air, taken in through tiny openings in the leaf called stomata

## What plants make
Inside the leaf, these three ingredients are combined to make:
- **Glucose** — a sugar. This is the plant's food, used for energy and growth.
- **Oxygen** — released into the air. Almost every animal on Earth, including us, depends on this oxygen to breathe.

## Why leaves are green
Leaves contain a green pigment called **chlorophyll**. Chlorophyll is what captures sunlight. It absorbs red and blue light and reflects green light — which is why leaves look green to our eyes.

## Where it happens
Photosynthesis happens mostly in the leaves, inside tiny structures called **chloroplasts**. A single leaf can contain millions of them. The plant's roots and stem support the leaf and deliver the water it needs.

## Why it matters
Photosynthesis is the starting point of almost every food chain on Earth. Plants use the glucose they make to grow, and animals eat the plants. Even the oxygen we breathe comes from photosynthesis — most of it from ocean algae and land plants. Without photosynthesis, life as we know it wouldn't exist.

## Try this at home
Place one leafy plant in a sunny window and another in a dark closet. Water both the same way for a week. Compare the leaves after seven days. What changed? What does this tell you about what plants need?
