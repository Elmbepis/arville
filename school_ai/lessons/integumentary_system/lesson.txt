# The Integumentary System
Your skin, hair, and nails — the body's outer covering.

## What the integumentary system is
The **integumentary system** is your body's outer layer — the parts that face the outside world. It includes:
- **Skin** — the largest organ of your body
- **Hair** — on your head, arms, and elsewhere
- **Nails** — on fingers and toes
- **Sweat glands** — tiny glands inside your skin
- **Oil glands** — keep skin and hair from drying out

The word "integumentary" comes from the Latin word for "covering."

## The skin: your largest organ

If you could spread out an adult's skin, it would cover almost **2 square meters** — about the size of a bedsheet. It weighs about **4 kilograms** — heavier than your brain.

Skin has **three layers**:

### 1. Epidermis (outer layer)
- The part you see and touch
- Made of flat cells that constantly flake off and are replaced
- Contains **melanin** — a pigment that gives skin its color and protects against the sun
- Has no blood vessels — that's why a shallow cut doesn't bleed

### 2. Dermis (middle layer)
- Contains **blood vessels**, **nerves**, **hair follicles**, **sweat glands**, and **oil glands**
- Feels touch, pressure, pain, and temperature
- Where goosebumps come from — tiny muscles pull hair upright

### 3. Hypodermis (inner layer)
- Made mostly of fat
- Cushions muscles and bones
- Keeps you warm
- Stores energy

## What skin does

Skin is more than just a covering. It:
- **Protects** — blocks germs, dirt, and harmful sun rays
- **Senses** — lets you feel touch, heat, cold, pain
- **Controls temperature** — sweating cools you; blood vessels widen or narrow to release or save heat
- **Removes waste** — sweat carries out small amounts of salt and urea
- **Makes vitamin D** — when sunlight hits your skin, your body makes this important vitamin
- **Heals itself** — cuts and scrapes repair on their own

## Hair
Hair grows out of tiny pockets in the skin called **follicles**.
- Hair on your head protects from sun and cold
- Eyelashes and eyebrows keep dust and sweat out of your eyes
- Hair in your nose filters dust from the air
- Each hair grows about 1 cm per month

## Nails
Nails are made of a hard protein called **keratin** — the same thing as hair.
- Protect the tips of your fingers and toes
- Help you pick up small things
- Show signs of health — pale or ridged nails can be a sign of illness

## Sweat glands
You have about **2 to 4 million** sweat glands. When you're hot or nervous, they release sweat onto your skin. As the sweat evaporates, it cools you down.

## Keeping your integumentary system healthy
- **Wash regularly** — removes dirt, oil, and germs
- **Wear sunscreen** — protects against harmful UV rays
- **Drink water** — keeps skin healthy from the inside
- **Eat well** — vitamins and healthy fats help skin and hair
- **Don't scratch bug bites or rashes** — can cause infection
- **Trim your nails** — keeps them clean and prevents scratching

## Try this at home
Look closely at the back of your hand with a magnifying glass. Can you see fine hairs? Pores? Tiny lines and ridges? Your skin is doing millions of small jobs every second — and you don't have to think about any of them.
