# The Skeletal System
The bones that hold you up and protect what's inside.

## What the skeletal system does
Your **skeleton** is made of **bones**. An adult has **206 bones**. They work together to:
- **Support** your body and give it shape
- **Protect** important organs
- **Help you move** by working with muscles
- **Make blood cells** inside the bone marrow
- **Store minerals** like calcium and phosphorus

Without a skeleton, you'd be a soft pile on the floor.

## Types of bones
Bones come in different shapes for different jobs:

- **Long bones** — arms, legs. Strong and good for movement.
- **Short bones** — wrists, ankles. Small and sturdy.
- **Flat bones** — skull, ribs, shoulder blades. Protect organs.
- **Irregular bones** — spine, jaw. Odd shapes for specific jobs.

## Key bones in your body

- **Skull** — protects your brain
- **Spine (backbone)** — supports your body and protects the spinal cord
- **Ribs** — form a cage around your heart and lungs
- **Sternum (breastbone)** — in the middle of your chest
- **Pelvis** — supports your lower body
- **Femur** — thigh bone; the longest and strongest bone
- **Humerus** — upper arm bone
- **Patella** — kneecap

## Joints
A **joint** is where two bones meet. Joints let your body bend.

- **Hinge joints** — knees and elbows; bend like a door
- **Ball-and-socket joints** — shoulders and hips; move in all directions
- **Pivot joints** — neck; let you turn your head
- **Fixed joints** — skull; don't move

## What bones are made of
Bones are not dry and dead — they're alive:
- **Outer layer** — hard compact bone
- **Inner layer** — spongy bone, light but strong
- **Bone marrow** — soft center where blood cells are made
- **Cartilage** — smooth, rubbery tissue at the ends of bones; protects joints

Babies have about **300 bones**, but some fuse together as they grow, leaving adults with 206.

## Keeping bones healthy
- **Calcium** — from milk, cheese, leafy greens, sardines
- **Vitamin D** — from sunlight and some foods; helps calcium absorb
- **Exercise** — running, jumping, and sports make bones stronger
- **Avoid falls** — protect your bones from breaks

## Try this at home
Feel the bones in your own body:
- Touch your skull — hard, round, protective
- Find your ribs — a cage around your chest
- Bend your elbow — that's a hinge joint
- Move your shoulder in a circle — that's a ball-and-socket joint
- Touch your kneecap — that's the patella
