# Earth's Motions
How the Earth's movement creates day and night, seasons, and eclipses.

## Two ways Earth moves
The Earth is always moving in two ways:
1. **Rotation** — spinning on its axis (like a spinning top)
2. **Revolution** — traveling around the Sun (like a car on a racetrack)

Both motions affect life on Earth in important ways.

## Rotation: day and night
The Earth spins on an imaginary line called its **axis**. One full spin takes about **24 hours** — one day.

As the Earth spins, one side faces the Sun and the other side faces away. The side facing the Sun has **daytime**. The side facing away has **nighttime**.

The Sun isn't actually moving across the sky — it just looks that way because we're spinning. The Sun "rises" in the east and "sets" in the west because of how the Earth turns.

## Revolution: seasons
The Earth also travels around the Sun. One full trip takes about **365 days** — one year.

The Earth's axis isn't straight up and down — it's **tilted** about 23.5 degrees. Because of this tilt, different parts of the Earth get more direct sunlight at different times of the year.

- When the Northern Hemisphere is tilted toward the Sun, it's **summer** there.
- At the same time, the Southern Hemisphere is tilted away — so it's **winter** there.

The Philippines is near the equator, so it doesn't get four seasons like some countries. Instead, we have a **wet season** and a **dry season**, based on rainfall and wind patterns.

## The Sun-Earth-Moon system
The Moon also orbits the Earth. It takes about **29 days** for the Moon to go around us once. As it orbits, we see different parts of its lit side — these are the **phases of the Moon**.

- **New Moon** — the Moon is between us and the Sun, so we see its dark side
- **Full Moon** — the Earth is between the Sun and Moon, so we see the fully lit side
- **Crescent** and **gibbous** phases in between

## Eclipses
Sometimes the Sun, Earth, and Moon line up in a special way, causing an **eclipse**.

### Solar eclipse
When the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth, it blocks the Sun's light for a short time. It becomes dark during the day. **Never look directly at a solar eclipse without special glasses** — it can damage your eyes.

### Lunar eclipse
When the Earth passes between the Sun and Moon, the Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. The Moon looks reddish — sometimes called a "blood moon."

## Why this matters
The motions of the Earth and Moon affect:
- When we sleep and wake (day and night)
- When crops grow (seasons)
- Fishing and tides (Moon's gravity)
- Cultural events and calendars based on the Moon

Understanding these motions helps us plan farming, travel, and celebrations.

## Try this at home
Use a globe (or a ball) and a lamp. Turn off the lights. The lamp is the Sun. Slowly spin the globe — see how one side is lit while the other is dark. That's day and night.
