# Plant Adaptations
How plants survive in tough environments.

## What is a plant adaptation?
An **adaptation** is a special feature that helps a plant survive in its environment. Plants cannot move to escape bad conditions, so they have to adapt where they grow. Over thousands of years, plants that live in similar places have developed similar adaptations.

## Surviving without water
Deserts get very little rain. Plants that live there — called **xerophytes** — have special ways to survive:

- **Cacti** store water in thick, fleshy stems
- **Spines** instead of leaves reduce water loss and protect from animals
- **Waxy coatings** on leaves keep water from escaping
- **Long, deep roots** reach far underground for water
- **Short growing seasons** — desert plants bloom and seed quickly after rain

## Surviving in water
Plants that live in water — called **hydrophytes** — face different problems: too much water, not enough air.

- **Floating leaves** like lily pads get sunlight at the water's surface
- **Hollow stems** carry air down to roots underwater
- **Thin, feathery roots** absorb nutrients directly from the water
- **Mangroves** have special roots that stick out of the water like snorkels — called **pneumatophores** — to breathe air

## Surviving in cold places
- **Pine trees** have needle-shaped leaves that lose less water and survive snow
- **Conical shape** lets snow slide off branches
- **Dark green color** absorbs more sunlight

## Storing food underground
Some plants store food in underground parts so they can survive the dry season or winter:

- **Tubers** — potatoes store starch underground
- **Bulbs** — onions and garlic have layers of stored food
- **Rhizomes** — ginger and bamboo spread underground
- **Corms** — taro and crocus store food in a thick base

## Climbing and support
Vines don't have strong stems. Instead, they climb:

- **Tendrils** curl around supports — like in peas and grapes
- **Aerial roots** cling to tree trunks — like in some orchids
- **Thorns and hooks** attach to other plants — like in climbing roses

## Protection from animals
- **Thorns and spines** — cacti, bougainvillea, and roses
- **Bitter or poisonous chemicals** — some plants taste bad or are toxic
- **Sticky sap** — traps insects that try to eat the plant
- **Hard shells** — coconuts have tough outer husks

## Catching food
A few plants are **carnivorous** — they catch and digest insects to get nutrients from soil-poor environments:

- **Venus flytraps** snap shut on flies
- **Pitcher plants** have slippery tubes insects fall into
- **Sundews** have sticky hairs

## Try this at home
Look at three plants around your home or school. For each one, ask: What is special about its leaves, stems, or roots? Does it have thorns? Thick leaves? Waxy surface? Every feature is an adaptation.
