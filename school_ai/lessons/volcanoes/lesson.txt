# Volcanoes
How they form, why they erupt, and how to stay safe.

## What is a volcano?
A **volcano** is an opening in the Earth's surface where hot melted rock, ash, and gas can escape from deep underground. When pressure builds up, the volcano **erupts** — shooting material high into the air.

## What's inside the Earth?
The Earth has layers:
- **Crust** — the thin outer shell we live on
- **Mantle** — a thick layer of hot, slowly-moving rock
- **Outer core** — liquid metal
- **Inner core** — solid metal

Deep in the mantle, temperatures are so high that rock melts into a thick liquid called **magma**. Magma is lighter than the surrounding rock, so it rises. When it reaches the surface, we call it **lava**.

## How volcanoes form
Most volcanoes form where **tectonic plates** meet. These are huge slabs of rock that make up the Earth's crust. They float on the mantle and move very slowly — a few centimeters per year.

There are three places volcanoes form:
1. **Divergent boundaries** — where plates pull apart
2. **Convergent boundaries** — where one plate slides under another
3. **Hotspots** — where magma rises through the middle of a plate

The Philippines sits along the **Ring of Fire** — a chain of volcanoes around the Pacific Ocean. That's why we have so many volcanoes.

## Types of volcanoes
- **Active** — has erupted recently and may erupt again (Mayon, Taal, Pinatubo)
- **Dormant** — hasn't erupted in a long time but could still wake up
- **Extinct** — scientists believe it will never erupt again

## Materials from eruptions
Volcanoes release many things:
- **Lava** — hot melted rock flowing down the slope
- **Ash** — fine gray powder that can travel far
- **Rock fragments** — small to large pieces of rock
- **Gases** — carbon dioxide, sulfur dioxide, water vapor
- **Pyroclastic flows** — fast-moving clouds of hot gas and rock (very dangerous)

## PHIVOLCS and alert levels
In the Philippines, **PHIVOLCS** (Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology) monitors volcanoes and issues **alert levels** from 0 to 5:
- **Level 0** — quiet
- **Level 1** — slight unrest
- **Level 2** — increasing unrest
- **Level 3** — dangerous tendency toward eruption
- **Level 4** — imminent eruption
- **Level 5** — eruption in progress

When PHIVOLCS raises the alert level, people in danger zones are evacuated.

## Safety: before, during, and after

### Before an eruption
- Know your evacuation route
- Prepare an emergency kit (water, food, medicine, flashlight, radio)
- Follow PHIVOLCS advisories

### During an eruption
- Stay indoors if ash is falling
- Wear a mask or wet cloth over your nose and mouth
- Close windows and doors
- Do not drive unless necessary

### After an eruption
- Wait for official clearance before returning
- Clean ash from roofs carefully (it can collapse under its weight)
- Avoid areas near rivers and valleys — lahars (mudflows) can still happen

## Why volcanoes matter
Despite the danger, volcanoes are important:
- **Fertile soil** — volcanic ash makes some of the richest farmland in the world
- **Geothermal energy** — heat from underground makes electricity
- **Minerals** — gold, silver, copper come from volcanic areas
- **Tourism** — many people visit volcanoes for their beauty

## Try this at home
Mix baking soda and vinegar in a small container. Watch it bubble and overflow — that's a simple model of how gas and pressure escape from a volcano.
