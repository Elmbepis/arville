Wafer Crystal Orientation

          

When a crystal is grown, layers of atomic planes are added successively at the liquid-solid interface.  Semiconductor wafers are usually built from {111} and {100} crystals.  To understand what these numbers mean, please refer to the article on Miller Indices.

   

Crystal growth is easiest in the <111> directions for both silicon and GaAs since the {111} planes are most closely packed.  This is the reason why {111} wafers are commonly used in the production of bipolar integrated circuits.

  

Low-threshold MOS circuits and low-noise operational amplifiers, on the other hand, are usually built on {100} silicon wafers.  Furthermore, crystal growth in the <100> direction is highly preferred for GaAs wafers, since these cleave more readily among the {110} planes, yielding rectangular dice with parallel faces.

  

During <100> crystal growth, care must be taken in order to prevent inadvertent thermal or mechanical shock, which can result in orientation changes within the crystal by twinning.  This causes the growth to subsequently proceed in a different direction.
