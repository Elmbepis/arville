Won't you feel hopeless and scared if a big semiconductor company spends

a huge chunk of money to buy something from your competitor?

 

The biggest spenders I've seen in my life are semiconductor companies.  Every year, engineers and managers are asked to come up with a budget plan to buy things that will make their company better.  Money is no object. Thus, it is not unusual for hundreds of machines costing $200K each to appear in these plans.  Year in and year out.  For the various international factories of the companies. 

 

Which is why if a big semiconductor company decides to buy from your competitor and all other companies follow, your business is toast.  Period.  So the last thing you want to do is hand your competitor an opportunity to get noticed by a big customer.  You must always give every such opportunity to yourself, not to your competitor.

 

Now imagine what life would be like if all these companies were buying from you instead of your competitor. 

 

But  before you can even start building a good relationship with big companies, you must first catch their attention.  We will tell you shortly how.

 

Just a few years ago, there was this fragile-looking lady whose only product was anti-ESD gowns.  She probably had these sewn in her garage, for all we know.  She started small, loading her merchandise in her old Kia Pride for her deliveries.  But soon she was able to get a contract with the company I was with, and many other companies nearby.  Today, she drives around in a fancy car and owns many properties.  Truth is, her story isn't unique in the big spending world of semiconductor manufacturing.

  

Anybody can have her success, but not everyone will.  After all, not everyone has what it takes nor the luck to get noticed by big corporate spenders.  So you ask yourself, how do you catch the attention of the big wigs of large semiconductor companies, all of whom are always too busy to stop and listen?

 

Well, you don't.  The people you want to notice you are not the big bosses.  The people you want to notice you are the engineers and managers who will use your products and services.  Why?  Because they will be the ones to draw up the big budget plan for next year and ask for money to buy 'things' that will make their departments perform better.  They are the 'experts' who will tell the top executives that they want to buy from you.  In short, they decide what to buy, and the bosses just approve them.

   

Now here's the good news. EESemi.com, with all its unique and highly specialized and very informative pages, attracts many of these engineers and managers as daily readers.  They visit us everyday to get information that will help them with their jobs.  The loyalty of these 'decision makers' helped us become a leading website on semiconductor manufacturing. The reason they keep on coming back to our site is because we give them information that can only be found in our pages.

  

Then it hit us. What if we can use our web pages to provide a way for these engineers and managers to see what other companies have to offer during their daily visits to our website?  Wouldn't we be of better service to them if we expose them to more products and services and help them make better decisions about their purchases?

 

On the other hand, if you are a supplier of products and services to semiconductor companies, wouldn't you be glad if we could make our readers see your company logo everyday and constantly remind them of the products you carry?  When it's time to do their budget planning once again, they will remember you and hopefully buy from you.
